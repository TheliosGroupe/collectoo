# ADR-0014: Remittances, commission, invoices and own cash (extends ADR-0012, ADR-0013)

- Status: Accepted (the funds ledger implemented in M2c-1, identities and terms in M2c-2, the engines in M2c-3; runs, invoices, documents and transfers follow in M2c-4 … M2c-7, each adding its section)
- Date: 2026-11-09
- Design: `docs/design/m2c-remittances.md` (three-design panel, critiques and a judge; legal texts re-read on LibreJustice on 2026-11-09). Founder answers on 2026-11-09: all 15 defaults accepted (« All ok », design §24).
- Supersedes ADR-0013's sentence « Only M2c `commission` will be exempt » (from I2): **no entry is exempt** from fund closure.

## Context
M2b books what debtors pay onto the account dedicated to funds collected for creditors (CPCE R124-2) and what each client is owed (4670). M2c pays clients (R124-6, one month), takes the servicer's commission by compensation when the mandate allows it, invoices it, and moves the servicer's own money (fees on the dedicated account, top-ups, sweeps, advances when a remitted collection comes back). The ledger must show at every date whose money is on the dedicated account, never let one client's money serve another client or the servicer (C. pén. 314-1, C. civ. 1993), and keep 5120 equal to the bank (I9).

## Decision

### Two funds and one crossing (design §0, §2 #1, §5)
- **The dedicated-account fund** (`ledger_book.money_of = 'client'`): 5120 `client_money_cash`, 4710 `unallocated_payment`, 4670 `client_payable` and the new **4679 `due_to_servicer`** « Sommes revenant au cabinet sur le compte dédié » (pooled per servicer and currency). −4679 is the servicer's money on the dedicated account at every date; +4679 what the servicer owes it.
- **The servicer's fund** (`money_of = 'servicer'`): **5122 `own_cash`** (one ledger account per `bank_account.purpose = 'own'`; it records only the movements the dedicated account shows and client payments ops prove — it is not the own account's bank balance), **4111 `commission_receivable`**, **4112 `client_advance_receivable`** (per client), **44571 `vat_collected`**, **44574 `vat_pending`**, **4689 `due_from_client_funds`**, 7060 `servicer_revenue` (no longer client-visible), **6270 `bank_fees`**, **6780 `servicer_losses`** (pooled).
- **Every entry closes each fund per currency (I2′)** and **4679 + 4689 = 0 in every entry (I12)**: money crosses between the funds only through this mirror pair, as 1100 / 3100 mirror each other. Checked at commit by `private.ledger_entry_verify` (`LG001`) and fail-fast in `private.ledger_post_set`. I8′ (5120 + 4710 + 4670 + 4679 = 0 per servicer and currency) follows; the nightly checks verify it, the servicer's fund and the mirror over all postings.

### Thirteen entry types with fixed shapes (design §5.2; `ledger_entry_type.allowed_books`, I3′)
`commission_invoice`, `commission_credit_note`, `client_settlement`, `remittance`, `servicer_sweep`, `servicer_top_up`, `bank_fee`, `client_advance`, `servicer_loss`, `client_payment_own`, `client_payment_dedicated`, `client_refund_own`, `advance_write_off`.
- Each has its books and its shape (+ debit / − credit), one currency, checked at commit (`LG001`); with I2′ and I12 the shape fixes every amount (a sweep's four lines are all S). No M2c type touches 1100 or 3100 (L111-8) nor 4710 (unallocated money is never remitted: M2b founder #8, not the law); 7060 is reached only by the two commission types, against the same client's 4111; no M2b type reaches a servicer-fund book or 4679 (I10′).
- Each names its document (`source_type` / `source_id`, C. com. R123-174, CHECK constraint): `commission_invoice`, `remittance_run`, `bank_transfer` or `bank_transaction`, `client_advance`, `document`.
- **Never reversed** (`LG003`): the two commission types (a credit note corrects an invoice), `client_settlement` (it survives a returned payout) and `advance_write_off`. The others are reversed with a motif.
- `private.ledger_post` posts corrections of claims only (`adjustment`); every payment and fund entry goes through `private.ledger_post_set`, which derives the client of client books (4670, 4111, 4112, 7060) and refuses a client on an entry without client lines, and puts 5120 only on an active `client_funds` account and 5122 only on an active `own` account (`LG002`; the ledger account guard too).

### Locks (design §5.5)
- The **allocation lock** `payments:<org>` is taken for every entry with a 4670 line, decided by its lines and not its type (and, as before, for the types that lower a payment or a receivable). With `ledger_reverse`, the only writers of 4670 hold it, so the pending queue read under it is exactly the committed set.
- The **remit lock** `remit:<org>` is taken by every writer of 4679 (`ledger_post_set`, `ledger_reverse` when the reversed entry moves 4679). Order: allocation lock → remit lock → invoice counter (M2c-4).

### Each client's 4670 never in debit, and the pending queue (design §5.4 I13, §9.1)
- `private.client_payable_balance` keeps Σ4670 per (client, currency), all dates, upserted by an ALWAYS statement trigger on `ledger_posting` (one hash join of the statement's new postings with the few 4670 accounts, through a partial index). A deferred constraint trigger refuses a commit leaving it in debit: **new SQLSTATE `LG008`** (Python `ClientMoneyShortError`). A path that forgets to cover a shortfall (M2c-7's advance after a remitted reversal, M2c-6's booking-time cover of a payout overrun) fails at commit instead of using another client's money. `private.ledger_check_now()` runs the ledger's commit checks inside a call (the debit job then sends a refused line to ops). The deferred checks run as their owner (a command's commit runs them as the API's role).
- The same trigger feeds **`private.remittance_pending`**: every entry that moves 4670 and is a remittance item — `allocation`, `forward`, their reversals, `client_advance` and its reversal, the reversal of a `remittance` (`payout_returned`); M2c-6 inserts unplanned payouts itself. Settlements and planned remittances are not items. Amount: what the entry adds to what the client is owed. A run (M2c-4) reserves its items and deletes them when it freezes its lines. Back-filled by the migration (none in production). Nightly: I13 (running balance = postings, never in debit) and, before runs exist, I14 (what each client is owed = its pending items).

### Bank accounts (design §2 #13, §5.3; founder #14)
- `bank_account.purpose` gains **`own`**: the servicer's own account, no statement feed (its statements are not imported: a statement row on it is refused). A second **active** `client_funds` account in a currency is refused by `register_bank_account` (a servicer that already had two keeps them; M2c-4 blocks its runs); one account per currency is the founder's choice, not a rule of R124-2. An IBAN is registered for one purpose only, in any currency: the dedicated account is never also the servicer's own account. `close_bank_account` refuses an account with a transfer in flight from or to it.

### Our own transfers at import (design §12)
- New `bank_transaction.kind`s: **`transfer_out`** (a debit carrying our `RMT…` / `SWP…` end-to-end id, or to a registered own IBAN), **`own_transfer_in`** (a credit carrying our `TOP…` id, or from a registered own IBAN — **never automatically a debtor payment**: ops book it as a top-up or release it as a debtor's payment the servicer relays, below), **`client_transfer_in`** (a credit from a client's registered payout IBAN; the list is empty until M2c-2 registers payout accounts). Our ids are the prefix and exactly 12 upper-case hex digits (`TOPAZ-…` stays a debtor's). camt.053 `NtryDtls/Btch/PmtInfId` is kept (`payment_info_id`, for M2c-6's batch bookings).
- The worker's parsers classify with the servicer's IBANs (`private.statement_classification_ibans`); `private.ingest_statement` classifies again under its lock with the SQL twin `private.bank_line_refined_kind` (a parity test compares them) and keeps the stage rows in step, so a stale list never makes a payment of our own money, and a held duplicate keeps that reading.

### Bank lines booked in the funds (design §6.1 `bank_line_booking`, §12, §13)
- `bank_line_booking(bank_transaction_id, seq, kind, transfer_id, entry_id, amount_minor, method, reason, decided_by)`: one bank line → n entries of the funds; append-only; a guard checks the line, the entry (type, date, bank account, document) and the transfer; at commit Σ bookings + the line's M2b `cash_out` (if any) = the line's amount. `bank_unposted` (I9) counts booked lines as in the ledger.
- **Bank fees** (`bank_fee`: +4679 / −5120 ‖ +6270 / −4689): the servicer's cost (founder #9), never a client's. The debit job books automatically a debit the bank codes as its charge (camt.053 BkTxCd sub-family `CHRG` or `FEES`) — M2b fee lines included, the migration queues the job; ops book other debits as fees (« Frais bancaires »). Only a line read as an other debit is a fee: our transfers out never are (M2c-6 books them).
- **Top-ups** (`servicer_top_up`: +5120 / −4679 ‖ +4689 / −5122): ops order one from the own account (`request_top_up`, a `bank_transfer` of kind `top_up` with our `TOP…` id, typed in the bank; `cancel_top_up` while pending). The debit job books the credit carrying that id, of that amount, on that dedicated account; ops book a credit from a registered own IBAN that no order matches. The automatic top-up file is M2c-7.
- **A debtor's payment relayed by the servicer** (design §12, ledger critique #8; review fix): a debtor paid the servicer's own account and the servicer passes the money on to the dedicated account. `public.release_own_credit(key, line, reason)` (admin or ops manager at aal2, the reason required and kept) takes only an unbooked, unlinked `own_transfer_in` credit on an active `client_funds` account of the caller's servicer and books it as a debtor payment, as a released duplicate credit is: a `payment` (payer « Versement relayé par le cabinet », `unmatched`, review `no_match`, so it waits in « Paiements à traiter » for ops to assign it) and its `cash_in` at the line's booking date. Refusals: `not_own_credit`, `already_booked`, `already_linked`, `reason_required` (`23514`). Whether the earlier receipt on the own account matters for R124-2 and R124-6 is OQ-69.
- **The servicer's loss** (`servicer_loss`: +4679 / −5120 ‖ +6780 / −4689; OQ-34, founder #8): a return larger than what is left of its payment is linked by ops as the payment's M2b return of what is left (its allocations reversed at the return date) plus a loss booking of the excess. Never charged to the client or another client; recovery from the payer is an ops matter.
- Our transfers out and credits from a client are left to ops with why (`bank_debit_review`: `top_up_not_found`, `top_up_differs`, `client_credit`, `own_transfer_out`) until M2c-6 / M2c-7 book them; meanwhile ops may explain them (an I9 gap, visible). They are never linked as a payment's return or our refund (`not_a_return`), and « À classer » does not offer it.

### Read access (design §6.2; compliance critique C6)
Every book without a portfolio is named in the policies: the two funds' books are read by admins and ops managers at aal2; a client reads only its own 4670, and of the entries without a portfolio only its own `forward`, `reversal`, `remittance`, `client_settlement` and `client_advance`. Collectors and clients no longer read 7060 (the `staff_org_ids` / `client_visible` path is closed). `bank_transfer` and `bank_line_booking`: money staff; the running balance and the queue: nobody through the API.

## Consequences
- 5120 stays equal to the bank: fees and top-ups are booked from the bank's lines, not estimated; the acceptance « 5120 = bank after fees and top-ups » is checked end to end (`worker/tests/test_funds_integration.py`).
- Whatever the servicer owes the dedicated account (a fee, a loss, later an advance) is visible on 4679 at once and nets against the servicer's settled commission before a sweep (M2c-6) or is covered by a top-up.
- **Until M2c-7 wires the advance into every reversal path, a reversal of a remitted entry fails closed with `LG008` only when it would leave the client's whole 4670 in debit** (the check is on the client's balance per currency, all entries). When the client has other collections pending, the reversal nets against them in the pending queue, the silent compensation design §13 #1 and founder #7 exclude. M2c-7 brings the advance (`cover_remitted_reversals`) and its nightly backstop; until it is merged there is no real remittance (design §21), and no M2c-1 command posts a `remittance`.
- Cost: the 4670 trigger adds one pass over each posting statement's new rows (measured in `docs/benchmarks/remittances.md`); the per-entry commit check adds one keyed pass for I2′ / I12.

## Review fixes (M2c-1 review, 2026-11-09)
Confirmed findings of the five-lens review (security, money, legal, tests, performance), fixed in this slice:
- **One decision per bank line** (security #0, money #0): the debit links (M2b path) and the funds' bookings (M2c path) take a common lock on the line before they read whether it is linked or booked, so a line is never both linked and booked (5120 never taken down twice).
- **A debtor's payment relayed by the servicer** (money #1, legal #0): `public.release_own_credit` (above); the copy no longer says a credit from the own account is never a debtor's payment, only that it is never taken for one automatically (OQ-69).
- **One purpose per IBAN** (legal #2): `register_bank_account` refuses an IBAN already registered for this servicer with the other purpose, in any currency.
- **Top-up alert** (legal #3): a nightly alert (`servicer_shortfall`, design §15) when the servicer owes the dedicated account (4679 in debit) beyond the top-ups ordered.
- **No fee booking of our transfers out** (legal #5): `fee` only on a line read as an other debit (the guard keeps `return` for M2c-6's charges on a returned payout).
- **Plan robustness** (performance #0–#2, ARCHITECTURE §4): the funds pass's anti-joins on `bank_line_booking` and `bank_debit_link` and its lookups of `bank_duplicate_resolution` and `bank_transfer` are keyed probes (`OFFSET 0`, no `LIMIT 1` over a filtered join); `post_bank_bookings` reads the chunk's lines by keyed laterals and locks the bank accounts from them. The deferred `LG008` check fires only when a client's running balance goes into debit, no longer once per upsert when a loop posts single entries (performance #3: 10,000 single-row upserts 1.57 s → 0.46 ms of checks); the upserts themselves stay one per posting statement (a set-based `ledger_reverse`, one upsert per client and chunk, is left for when return volumes need it: ~0.19 s per 4,000 reversals of one client).
- **Top-up lock** (security #1): `request_top_up` locks both accounts and checks them after the lock, so a concurrent close cannot leave a top-up in flight on a closed account.
- **Decided lines leave the queues** (tests #0, #2): a return booked entirely as the servicer's loss is no longer a debit candidate nor reviewed again by the job; a fee already booked is no longer listed in « À classer » as a possible refund.
- **Stronger pgTAP 017** (tests #3): client reads scoped client by client (A/B) and for a portfolio-restricted viewer — which found the client branch of the ledger read policies ignored portfolio restrictions: a member restricted to some portfolios no longer reads the client's 4670, client-level entries or `ledger_balance` (`private.client_whole_org_ids`, OQ-48 default); `fund_mirror` and `own_fund_identity` shown to fire on tampered postings.
- **« À classer »** (tests #1): no « Retour du paiement » nor refund link on our transfers out, our own credits or a client's credit (the database refuses them, `not_a_return`).
- **Law and our own rules apart** (legal #1, #6): the `LG008` consequence above states when it fails closed; « un seul compte dédié par devise » is the founder's choice (#14), not R124-2; COMPLIANCE CPCE-02 grounds only 1100/3100 on L111-8 (4710 is M2b founder #8).

Refuted at verification: M2b fee lines stuck as « expliquées » (money #2: M2b left them unlinked, so the job books the coded ones); a cap on manual top-ups (legal #4: neither the law nor the design asks for one, OQ-35).

## M2c-2: identities and terms (CPCE-03, M2-13 data; design §6.1–§6.2, §8.1–§8.2, §8.5, §9.1, §10, §14.2, §17)
Migration `20261111120000_identities_terms.sql` (forward-only; redefines `confirm_boarding_batch`, `complete_batch_confirmation`, `receipt_prepare`, `registered_client_ibans`, `close_bank_account` with their settings and grants).
- **Billing identity** (`billing_identity`, one per organization, the servicer and each client; legal name and SIREN stay on `organization`): legal form and capital, RCS city, registered office and billing address (`{line1, line2, postcode, city, country}`), VAT number (French key checked against the SIREN), professional, `established_in` derived from the registered office (mainland France: a French postcode outside 97x/98x; Corsica is mainland), in liquidation, EI (derived from the form), the client's acceptance of PDF invoices (CGI 289 VI). The servicer's is saved complete or not at all — SIREN, a legal form R123-238 lets us print (a servicer of another form cannot save it), the capital for SARL and sociétés par actions, RCS city, VAT number — because every document prints it. `set_billing_identity` (admin at aal2, compare and set on `version`). Staff read every identity of the servicer; a client member reads its own and its servicer's.
- **Billing settings** (`servicer_billing_settings`): invoice prefix (2–6 capitals), the own account due invoices are paid to (an active own account; `close_bank_account` refuses it while named), VAT on debits (242 nonies A 11° bis), the penalty text (default: the ECB rate + 10 points, L441-10 II; a free text until invoices print it: the « ≥ 3 × the legal rate » floor of an agreed rate is checked in M2c-5 with M3's legal-rate table), the bank cut-off per dedicated account. Money staff read, admins write.
- **Client documents**: the RIB, the signed mandate or an amendment, the evidence of an event, in the private bucket `documents` at `client/<servicer>/<client>/<uuid>.<pdf|png|jpg>`, uploaded by money staff from the browser (storage policy: that key, a new one) and registered with their SHA-256 (`register_client_document`). Read by money staff; the RIB and the mandate also by the client's administrators with access to the whole client (OQ-48 default); the evidence by money staff only. Served after the hash check.
- **Payout accounts under dual control** (founder #10; design §2.2 #16): `client_payout_account` holds the IBAN, BIC and holder as on the client's RIB (the payee name the bank checks). Money staff propose (`propose_payout_account`; never one of the servicer's own IBANs, never another client's current or proposed IBAN, one proposal at a time per client and currency). A **second person** confirms by typing the IBAN read on the RIB (compare and set): the client's administrator with access to the whole client, at aal2, in the client space, or another admin of the servicer at aal2; never the proposer (command and CHECK constraint), never an ops manager. A court-appointed account (the liquidator's, L641-9 I) — and, once a liquidation or an event naming a court-appointed payee is recorded for the client, every account of that client — is confirmed, rejected or retired by a second admin only, never by the client's users (« payout account refused: court_appointed », 42501; OQ-41 default; M2c-2 review). A confirmed account replaces the current one (one current account per client and currency); `reject_payout_account` (money staff or the client's administrator) and `retire_payout_account` (admin) need a reason; what was proposed never changes and the status only moves forward (ALWAYS guard). The page flags an active IBAN as « Nouvel IBAN » (« premier virement à vérifier ») until a payout of that client to that exact IBAN was booked and not returned (M2c-2 review); M2c-4 shows it on the run. Client users step up to aal2 on `/mfa` (opened to them, back to the page they came from). `private.registered_client_ibans` now returns every confirmed IBAN (current, returned or retired), so a credit from one is read as `client_transfer_in` at import — by the parser and by `ingest_statement` — and never as a debtor payment; a proposal is not yet the client's.
- **Client status events** (`client_status_event`, append-only, with evidence): proceeding opened, liquidation, death, guardianship, mandate ended (C. civ. 2003, C. com. L622-7, L641-9); for a liquidation the liquidator's court-appointed account may be named. Since the review, a mandate end or a guardianship, a death, or a liquidation keeps the client's portfolios from being confirmed (gate below), and a liquidation or a court-appointed payee leaves the client's payout accounts to a second admin (above). Their effects on runs and payouts come with M2c-4 and M2c-7 (OQ-41).
- **Mandate money terms** (R124-3 2°–4°): `mandate.insurance_clause_ref` (2°, `set_mandate_insurance_clause`, compare and set); per `mandate_terms` version the commission schedule (`commission-1`) and the remittance terms (`remittance-1`), each with its clause reference **in a column** (`commission_clause_ref`, `remittance_clause_ref`: one place, not repeated inside the documents; the netting and claw-back clauses are named inside the remittance terms), the signed document and `schedule_sha256`. The delay stays `remittance_max_days` (1–30), the one source. Validators: SQL (`private.commission_schedule_error`, `private.remittance_terms_error`, also CHECK constraints) and pydantic (`collectoo_worker.remittance.terms`; the JSON Schemas in `remittance/schemas/` are generated from the models and a test keeps them in step); both give the same verdict on 5,000 generated schedules and 5,000 generated terms, valid and invalid. Amounts and rates are strings; whole-rate tiers are not a kind; the last rule matches everything; ids and tier keys are distinct; a weekly frequency names a weekday 1–5; a due invoice is payable within 45 days (L441-10 I); `holdback` must be empty until M5.
- **Versions** (§6.1): a version in force (from ≤ Paris today) never changes — every column but its end, compared as a whole row so a later column is covered too — its end moves only to a day not yet passed, and it is never deleted; a version not yet in force may be superseded. A second version starts tomorrow (Europe/Paris) at the earliest, never today (never retroactive: versions apply by the day of the encaissement, so today's collections stay under the version in force; « mandate terms refused: starts_too_early »; M2c-2 review; a mandate's first version may start at its signature). `set_mandate_terms(key, mandate, valid_from > today, schedule, clause, terms, clause, document, max_days, expected_latest_id)` (admin at aal2): compare and set on the mandate's latest version as the page showed it; the version covering the day ends then, versions starting on or after it are superseded, the new one keeps the mandate's end and the covering version's plan envelope and settlement authority; claim types it names exist and portfolios it names are the mandate's. A mandate with versions is never deleted (`ON DELETE RESTRICT`). Back-fill: versions written before M2c-2 get one flat rule at their `commission_rate` and no clause references, so they read « incomplete » (none in production).
- **Confirmation gate** (§9.1; ops critique #3, compliance critique B8): `private.portfolio_confirmation_blockers` / `batch_confirmation_blockers` name, in order: `no_active_mandate`, the client's recorded events with their date (`client_mandate_ended:YYYY-MM-DD` for a mandate end or a guardianship, `client_deceased:…`, `client_in_liquidation:…`; M2c-2 review), `insurance_clause_missing`, `commission_schedule_missing`, `commission_clause_missing`, `remittance_terms_missing`, `remittance_clause_missing`, `terms_document_missing`, `servicer_identity_incomplete`, `client_identity_incomplete`, `client_not_professional`, `client_not_mainland_france`, `currency_not_eur`. `confirm_boarding_batch` refuses with the list; the confirmation job checks again (an identity or terms may change in between) and sends the batch back to `committed` with the reasons, nothing posted. The batch page lists them before « Confirmer »; `/backoffice/clients` and the client page show them per portfolio (`client_readiness`).
- **Receipts** (founder 2026-11-09, R123-237/238): `private.receipt_issuer` builds the issuer block from the identity; every new version's payload carries it and the worker renders template `quittance-fr-3` (the block above the parties). « What the receipt says changed » leaves the issuer out, so a new or changed identity alone never issues a version: receipts issued before keep `quittance-fr-2` and their bytes (its golden is unchanged; `quittance-fr-3` has its own). A stored receipt is regenerated with its own template version.
- **Pages** (French, phone-first): `/backoffice/parametres/facturation` (identity with the receipt block previewed, settings), `/backoffice/clients` (readiness per client) and `/backoffice/clients/[id]/reversement` (readiness, identity, documents, payout IBAN, mandates and their versions in words, events), `/client/mandat` (terms in words, « Confirmer le nouvel IBAN »; restricted users read that their access does not cover it). The terms are entered as structured fields plus the schedule as JSON, shown back in words; the editor with live examples from the engine (§17.4) comes with M2c-3's engine.

## Review fixes (M2c-2 review, 2026-11-10)
Confirmed findings of the five-lens review (security, money and terms, legal, tests and UI, performance), fixed in this slice. The database side follows one contract with the pages (1–5 below).
- **(1) Court-appointed payout accounts** (security #2, money #1, legal #0): once a liquidation, or any event naming a court-appointed payee, is recorded for the client, its own users can neither confirm nor reject nor retire its payout accounts, a court-appointed one never; only a second admin of the servicer acts (L641-9 I: the debtor's rights are exercised by the liquidator; OQ-41 default). Refusal « payout account refused: court_appointed » (42501). The client space shows no action on a court-appointed account and says why; the refusal reads in French.
- **(2) The gate reads the client's events** (legal #1, money #3): `client_mandate_ended` (a mandate end or a guardianship) and `client_deceased` (C. civ. 2003: the mandate ends), and `client_in_liquidation` — our rule, not the law (the liquidator may keep contracts in force, L641-11-1; OQ-41) — each with the event's date, in the same list as the other reasons; the request and the job's re-check both read them. A proceeding opened does not block (design §13 #9). The batch page, the client page (with a link to « Événements du client ») and the job's failure note word them with their date.
- **(3) New terms from tomorrow** (legal #2): a new version starts tomorrow (Europe/Paris) at the earliest, never today (design §8.1 « from a future date », founder #5); refusal « mandate terms refused: starts_too_early ». The form's earliest and default date is tomorrow. A mandate whose version in force is incomplete becomes ready the day its new version starts.
- **(4) A debtor's payment relayed by the client** (money #0): `release_own_credit` also releases an unbooked `client_transfer_in` credit (from a client's confirmed payout IBAN) as a payment, payer « Versement relayé par le client », unmatched, for ops to assign; same refusals. « Débits et retours » offers « Paiement d'un débiteur relayé par le client » on those lines, with the same sheet and errors; the sheet says not to use it for the client paying our invoice, nor for a payment already declared as made to the client directly.
- **(5) « Nouvel IBAN »** (security #3): true until a payout of that client to that exact IBAN was booked and not returned (per client, not per servicer; a returned payout no longer counts).
- **The client checks the IBAN against its own records** (security #1, medium): the client's confirmation screen no longer shows or links the proposal's RIB; the client types the IBAN of its account from its own documents.

## M2c-3: engines (M2-13; design §3, §8.3–§8.5, §9.2, §9.3, §18, §21)
Pure Python engines in `collectoo_worker.remittance`, the rule packs they read (`worker/rule_packs/`, ADR-0005), and migration `20261111150000_engines.sql` (forward-only; nothing earlier is redefined) for the data the database shares with them. Texts re-read on LibreJustice on 2026-11-10 and quoted in the packs: CGI 256 I, 269 1 a bis and 2 c, 278, 289 I 1 a (each « abrogé à compter du 1er janvier 2027 » by ordonnance 2025-1247, art. 9, 15, 49, except the paragraphs art. 15 maintains), their CIBS successors from 2027-01-01 (L211-4, L213-151, L214-6, L214-9, L216-30; ordonnance 2026-671, art. 19 I), CPCE R124-6, CPC 641, CMF L133-4 d and L133-13 I, C. trav. L3133-1.
- **Commission engine `commission-1`** (`commission.py`, §8.3; CALCULATION_RULES §7): per (file, tier key) the cumulative function F(c) = min(max(m, R(g(c))), ⌊c / (1 + τ)⌋), g flat, marginal or bracket; a line's commission is F_v(c after) − F_v(c before) under the version v in force on its encaissement date, lines in order of (encaissement date, effective date, posted at, entry id) (*superseded by the third review: event order*), after the history of earlier runs. Integers and exact decimals; one rounding half-up to the cent per value of F (the bands are summed exactly first). The basis is founder #4's: an allocation's 1100 credits per family filtered by `basis.families`, a forward only when its reason is listed, a reversal minus its original's (nothing back under `on_reversal: keep`), advances and payouts never. Rules are matched per family part; a forward has no family and skips rules filtering on families. An item with no file or no terms in force gets state `none`. Bad inputs (a negative cumulative basis, a missing bracket attribute, an entry twice, overlapping versions, a forward carrying families) raise `CommissionInputError` (M2c-4 keeps the commission pending). `inputs_hash` names every input (the same inputs in any order give the same hash).
- **Decisions where the design was open:**
  - **Across versions, K stays within [0, ⌊c / (1 + τ)⌋]** (superseded by the review fixes below: the commission is a function of the live lines). The design proves telescoping inside one version only. With a tier key continuing across versions, the differences of two versions' F can take a file's net commission below zero (a credit beyond what was charged) or above what the file brought in. The engine keeps each (file, tier key)'s net commission K in [0, ⌊c / (1 + τ)⌋]; inside one version the bound never binds (property test), and a file undone in full is back to 0 in every case. History therefore carries both c and K per (file, tier key).
  - **A line may have several parts** (one per rule its families reach, e.g. interest at 20 % and principal at 10 %): `remittance_line` (M2c-4) stores the parts (jsonb) rather than the single `tier_key` column §6.1 sketched.
  - **Bracket bounds are included** (« jusqu'à », as the terms page already words it): value ≤ bound picks the bracket.
  - **A forward's basis** is its 4670 amount; the families filter of the schedule does not apply to it (only `basis.forwards`).
  - **The cap's τ** is the VAT rate the run's invoices will bear (an input of the run).
- **Documents** (`documents.py`, §8.4; pure, posted and numbered by M2c-4; amended by the review fixes below: no netting across documents): per (mandate, file, tier key) the run's net Δ; positive keys make the mandate's invoice, lines grouped per (claim type, rule of the key's last part, families), with the base moved per rate band for the designation (242 nonies A 8°); invoice VAT = R(HT × rate); negative keys make its credit note, allocated LIFO on the earlier invoices that charged that key (each up to what it charged net of earlier credits), VAT per corrected invoice by cumulative difference at that invoice's rate; nothing when every Δ is 0. `after_issue` gives the charge history the next run's credit notes allocate against.
- **VAT** (`vat.py`, rule pack `vat/fr`): invoice VAT, credit-note VAT, and the VAT moved from 44574 to 44571 as a document is settled (§9.6: R(VAT × settled / TTC) − what moved before), all exact integers. `public.vat_rate` holds the pack's rows — the standard rate in mainland France, CGI 278 (2014-01-01 → 2027-01-01) then CIBS L213-151 — with their reference; a CI test checks the table equals the YAML; a guard lets an update only close an open row or record its review, once; no delete. `private.vat_rate_on(day)`. **Tripwire:** the 2027 row carries `review_due_on` 2026-12-01; from that day `worker/tests/test_vat.py` fails CI and `private.reference_data_alerts` returns `vat_pack_review` until a human re-reads the text and a migration records `reviewed_on` / `reviewed_by` (the build never marks it). Which date fixes the rate (fait générateur at the end of the period, 269 1 a bis, or exigibility) is OQ-70; the CIBS article replacing 269 1 a bis was not found (OQ-42).
- **Business calendar** (`calendar.py`, rule pack `remittance/fr`): FR-conservative = weekends + French public holidays (L3133-1) + TARGET closing days (Good Friday, 26 December: a product convention, not law, noted and not quoted); Easter by the Gregorian computus. `public.business_calendar` holds every day of the seeded horizon 2026-01-01 … 2031-12-31 with its French label and sources (the holiday rows generated from the pack, the weekends in SQL); append-only; CI checks it equals the Python calendar day for day. Both sides refuse a day outside the horizon. **Horizon check:** `private.reference_data_alerts` returns `calendar_horizon` when less than 12 months are seeded, and CI fails then (`test_deadline.py`, `test_engines_integration.py`). A paying bank's own closures come with M2c-6.
- **Deadline** (`deadline.py`, §9.2, founder #2) and its **SQL twin** `private.remittance_deadline(E, days, soon = 5, critical = 1)`: deadline = least(E + mandate days, E + one month per CPC 641), no extension; last business day ≤ deadline; latest execution = the business day before (L133-13 I); alert dates 5 and 1 business days before it. `private.encaissement_date`, `private.add_month_cpc641` (Postgres clamps `+ interval '1 month'` exactly as CPC 641 al. 2). Parity on 10,000 generated (E, delay) pairs, every holiday and two Januaries–Februaries (leap and common), and the same refusals near the horizon's ends; the SQL defaults of the margins are the pack's (checked by the parity). **Plans** (ARCHITECTURE §4): one keyed range scan of at most 41 days per call under `enable_seqscan = off`; pgTAP and the parity test check no sequential scan of the calendar. Callers compute one deadline per distinct (E, delay), not per line.
- **Schedule and guard** (`schedule.py`, §9.3, founder #1): the run days of a month — the 1st and the 16th (the pack's days), or the mandate's weekday — each moved to the next business day (a moved day may fall in the next month); the guard is due once the earliest latest execution date of the positive pending items is 5 business days away or less (the pack's margin, the same as `alert_soon`). Pure; the hourly job that applies them is M2c-4's.

## Review fixes (M2c-3 review, 2026-11-10)
Confirmed findings of the five-lens review (math, legal and VAT, deadline and calendar, performance, tests), fixed in this slice; the migration `20261111150000_engines.sql`, not yet applied in production, is edited in place.
- **The commission is a function of the live lines, across terms versions** (math #0, high). Per (file, tier key), the live lines in order of encaissement date, each priced by the version in force on its date (founder #5), each version's tiers continuing where the earlier versions' net basis ends: K = min(Σ_v [F_v(C_≤v) − F_v(C_<v)], ⌊C / (1 + τ)⌋), and each run charges K(after) − K(charged before). This is the design's « F_v(after) − F_v(before) » applied over the encaissement-date order of the whole live history rather than over the order runs arrive in, which is what made the old engine depend on the split into runs (three runs A, B, R-A charged 600,00 where B alone earns 400,00). So: the total after any partition into runs, in any order, equals one run over the same lines and one run over the surviving lines alone; undoing a collection gives back what it adds to the file — its own charge when nothing changed since, priced under its own version (a reversal's E is its original's); a full reversal returns 0; the cap binds on the file's total (the old clamp is gone). The history per (file, tier key) now carries the net basis per terms version (`KeyState.basis`), not one cumulative c. **Consequence, TO VALIDATE:** because tiers continue across versions on the net basis, undoing a collection made before a change of a marginal grid can move later collections into the new grid's lower tiers (`cross-version-cap`: the reversal gives back more than its own charge), and a collection dated before a change but booked after it can lower the commission (a credit note without a reversal). Both are rare (a back-dated or undone collection across a change of a marginal grid) and are what « tiers on what the file brought in, net of reversals » with « new rates only for the future » implies.
- **`keep` reversals leave the tier position** (math #1). Design §8.1 puts tiers on the basis « net of reversals » with no exception, so a kept reversal now lowers the net basis (later collections are priced where the undone money was, not in a lower tier) and what it would have given back is kept (κ, `KeyState.kept_minor`); the minimum per file is charged once (K = K° + max(0, κ − the minimum's top-up in K°)), capped on the gross basis (net plus kept, `kept_basis_minor`). The kept fee is what the reversal would have given back when it was processed, so under `keep` the split into runs can matter on a marginal grid (opt-in; OQ-39). *Third review: the order of the events matters, never the split — lines are taken in event order.*
- **TTC never above what the file brought in, across runs** (legal-vat #0 = tests #0). Each invoice rounds its own VAT (242 nonies A 11°), up to ½ cent more for a file's share, up to 1 cent after a partial credit; summed over runs the old cap let a small file be billed 3,01 € for 3,00 €. The cap is now also held in the cumulative domain: K ≤ ⌊(C − n) / (1 + τ)⌋, n the key's invoices open after the run (`KeyState.open_invoices`: the engine counts the run's own, the caller sets the real count from the charges, `documents.open_invoices`). Then the file's open HT plus its pro-rata share of those invoices' open VAT never exceeds C; for a file alone on its documents that is the TTC billed net of credit notes, to the cent (Hypothesis over any partition into runs). No exact bound can also be partition-invariant (an HT that depends on the lines alone can be split into runs whose invoices each round up), so the hold — at most ⌈n / (1 + τ)⌉ cents, only near the cap (the minimum on a small file), released when the basis leaves room — is the one place where runs matter; `schedule_commission` gives the partition-invariant K, `Part.held_minor` the hold.
- **No netting across documents** (legal-vat #1). Per (mandate, file, tier key) the parts that raise the commission go to the invoice, those that lower it to the credit note, which credits first the invoice that charged the collection a reversal undoes (`Line.original_entry_id`; `run_documents(…, line_invoices)`; M2c-4 has the line's invoice), the rest LIFO; each allocation names its invoice and line (`CreditAllocation.entry_id`). Only what was never invoiced nets inside the run: a reversal of a same-run collection, and a credit beyond what earlier invoices charge the key. Founder #6 (« un avoir sur la facture initiale »), CGI 289 I 5 (a modifying document « fait référence à la facture initiale de façon spécifique et non équivoque »; from 2027 CIBS L216-25 al. 2) and CGI 272 1 (« la rectification préalable de la facture initiale ») were re-read on LibreJustice and are quoted in `vat/fr.yaml`. Design §8.4 step 1 (net Δ per key) is amended accordingly.
- **The 2027 review covers every text the engine relies on** (legal-vat #2): each text not yet in force on the pack's `read_on` carries a review due before it applies — the rate row and CIBS L211-4, L214-6, L214-9, L216-25, L216-30, the maintained CGI 269 2 c, CGI 269 1 a bis (successor not found) and the unidentified successor of CGI 272 1 al. 3 (a note); the loader refuses a text without one and reviews of the same day recorded apart, so the rate row's review in `public.vat_rate` (the SQL alert) attests the whole pack; `vat.texts_due` drives the CI tripwire and names every text.
- **Delays the money cannot meet** (deadline #0): a mandate's delay of 1 to 5 days can put the latest execution on or before E (Fri 13/11/2026 under 2 days → Thu 12/11). Both twins return `unmeetable` (the SQL function gains the column), the parity covers 700 short-delay pairs; from 6 days on it never happens on 2026–2031 (property and pgTAP). M2c-4 records such lines as « délai intenable », not as operational breaches; whether the terms should refuse such a delay is the founder's call (OQ-71, default: warn).
- **Performance** (#0, #1): rate bands scaled once per (version, rule, file bracket), the version per (mandate, day) and the rule per (version, claim type, portfolio, family) cached, one pass over the versions per part, a streamed inputs hash. 50,000 lines (scratch benchmark of the review, same machine): one marginal part 1.77 → 1.51 s, two parts and two versions 2.85 → 2.63 s, with history 4.12 → 4.00 s, the hash included (0.48 → 0.25 s) — while doing more (the state across versions). The design's « ≈ 0.5 s » for the Python commission is not reachable in pure Python for two-part lines; `docs/benchmarks/remittances.md` records the figures and a unit guard (`test_commission_scale.py`, 50k lines, < 15 s) catches a regression before the M2c-7 scale run. `private.remittance_deadline` is declared `rows 1`.
- **Tests** (#1, #2): pgTAP `020` tries the review procedure on a synthetic next row and derives the 2026-12-01 alert from the table, so recording the real row's review keeps it green (checked); CALCULATION_RULES §7 no longer says a reversal gives back what its original earned.
- **For M2c-4** (the contract this fixes): persist per (file, tier key) the net basis per terms version, the kept basis and fee and K charged; the open-invoice count from the charges; each line's invoice; the parts (jsonb, with the hold). *Amended by the second review below: no open-invoice count; the engine reads the invoices' open charges.*
- **Second review (adversarial verification of these fixes, 2026-11-10)** — four defects, fixed:
  - **The minimum per file across versions** (medium). Each version's segment F_v(C_≤v) − F_v(C_<v) counted a minimum a new version introduced as already used by the collections made before it (400,00 € before a new 300,00 € minimum, 1 000,00 € after: 40,00 € charged; undoing the 400,00 € then invoiced 260,00 €, and a back-dated payment credited it). The minimum and the rounding now come out of the segments: K° = Σ_v [g_v(C_≤v) − g_v(C_<v)] exact, top = max(0, max over the versions v with b_v > 0 of m_v − K°_≤v), K = min(R(K° + top), ⌊C / (1 + τ)⌋) — each version's minimum a floor on the collections up to it, later collections earning their own rates on top; `keep` becomes K = max(R(K° + top), R(K°) + κ). Inside one version this is F exactly (every golden case unchanged). **Decision, TO VALIDATE:** the review proposed m* = the minimum of the version of the file's latest live collection; we did not take it, because with a later version without a minimum a payment would lower the commission (a credit note because money came in) and its reversal raise it — it breaks the property the review asked for, « with flat grids a reversal never raises K and a collection never lowers it », which holds with ours (Hypothesis, with and without VAT). Golden `minimum-new-version` (a minimum introduced, and one dropped). CALCULATION_RULES §7's « only across a change of a marginal grid » is now true.
  - **The rounding reserve's n** (low). n counted the run's invoice whenever the key was catching up a hold, even when its only part was a reversal: a kept reversal issued a credit note of 0,01 €, and with VAT a reversal gave back a cent more than its collection was charged. n is now **the key's invoices still open once the run's documents are issued**: the engine takes the largest V ≤ K whose own documents' n holds it (V ≤ ⌊(C − n(V)) / (1 + τ)⌋), simulating the key's side of the run's documents with the same code the documents use (`commission.key_side`, shared with `documents.py`) on what its earlier invoices still charge. The review's proposal (decide the +1 from the key's final invoice side, n + 1 first) is the special case where no credit closes an invoice; it was not enough for « the reversal of a collection charged in the previous run gives back exactly that charge »: that collection's invoice, closed by the reversal's credit note, must leave n too. So `compute` takes `charges` and `line_invoices` (the documents' history; checked against K charged, in the inputs hash) and `KeyState.open_invoices` is gone. A run in which a key has only reversals never releases its hold (no invoice from a reversal for the reserve's sake). Properties with τ = 0,20: under `keep` a reversal never issues a credit note; a collection charged in one run and undone in the next gives back exactly its charge — except, by at most one cent, a collection that lowered the commission across a change of marginal grid on a file at its hold (its undoing needs an invoice, whose VAT rounding the reserve must cover: no exact bound exists there).
  - **The hold on the parts** (low). The adjustment went to one part and could make a collection's part −0,01 €, so a run without a reversal issued a credit note. A negative adjustment is now taken from the key's positive parts, last to first, never below 0, and only what they cannot absorb from its reversals; a release goes to the last positive collection. Regression test (the review's 0,50 € + 0,01 € run) and property: no credit note and no negative part in a run without a reversal, inside one version.
  - **No negative band on an invoice line** (low; CGI ann. II 242 nonies A 8°, « la dénomination précise », re-read on LibreJustice in its version in force since 2025-01-01). `Part.tiers` now holds the part's own version's band only and `Part.retiering` the later versions' segments it moved. A part that re-tiers is split in the documents: its own band (R(Σ rate × base), by magnitude) on the commission line, the rest on a separate line « Régularisation de paliers (changement de conditions) » (`InvoiceLine.kind = regularisation`, net HT, no bands) when it raises the commission, credited when it lowers it — so an undone collection's own fee is credited on its original's invoice even when undoing it re-tiers later collections upward (a credit note and a regularisation invoice, never netted); a kept reversal's change is all regularisation. Golden `version-change-retiering` (upward and downward grids).
  - **Credits on the original's invoice first, strictly**: every reversal of the run is first allocated on its original's invoice, then what is left of every credit LIFO, so a credit with no original's invoice no longer takes the one another reversal's original was charged on (found by the review's property `credit_first`).
  - **Performance**: the 50k-line guard (case 2) 2.84–3.04 s for the engine, 1.04–1.33 s for the documents (2.54–2.56 s and 1.07–1.21 s before, same VM, same day); `docs/benchmarks/remittances.md`.
  - **For M2c-4** (replacing the contract above): persist per (file, tier key) the net basis per terms version, the kept basis and fee and K charged; per invoice and (file, tier key) what it charged and what credit notes took back (`EarlierCharge`), and each line's invoice — `compute` and `run_documents` both read them; the parts (jsonb, with the hold, the re-tiering and the kept flag); the invoice lines with their kind.
- **Third review (adversarial verification of the second round, 2026-11-10)** — four defects, fixed or bounded:
  - **Event order** (medium). Lines were processed in order of encaissement date, which a bounce shares with its collection: under `keep`, a bounce after a later collection came before it in one run and after it in two (1 % with a 300,00 € minimum: 310,00 € or 300,00 €; 10 % to 5 000,00 € then 5 %: 1 000,00 € or 750,00 €). A run now takes its lines in event order, (effective date, posted at, entry id) — `Line.event_key`, replacing `sort_key` — the order runs are cut in; the version is still chosen by each line's encaissement date. Any split of the same events into consecutive runs goes through the same states (property under `keep` and mandates whose clause changes across versions: tier positions, kept basis and fee, K; with no VAT the charge too). Under `credit` nothing in K changes. An entry back-dated before lines already remitted is taken after them, in its own run.
  - **A collection undone in its own run** (low). Under `credit`, a collection and its same-run reversal are taken out before pricing (they leave K unchanged) and both reported charged 0 with no parts, so no invoice charges a collection its own run undoes and no band moves twice (the review's « 10 % sur −32,40 € » for 0,99 €, and an invoice of 16,00 € in a run whose net was −84,00 €). The reversal must mirror its collection (same file and terms version, minus its commissionable basis; an entry undone once): otherwise `CommissionInputError`. Under `keep` they are priced (the clause keeps the fee; the kept reversal is a regularisation with no bands). So that a kept reversal falling between the two lines of a `credit` pair (a mandate whose clause changed) keeps the same fee as in any split, the pair's basis counts as live between them when that fee is taken (`_Key.transient`). The documents' commission line now sums only collections' own bands; a negative band raises `CommissionInputError` (the commission stays pending) instead of being issued. Properties: no negative band across versions, clauses and same-run reversals, with and without VAT; under `credit` no invoice charges a collection undone in its run. Golden `version-change-partial-reversal` (the one-run file: its pair now 0 / 0, no parts) and two committed tests that expected a history key after a same-run pair were updated.
  - **The undo bound** (low, documented, not removable under « never netted »). Across a change of tiered grid, on a file at its hold, an undo that needs a tier regularisation invoice adds one more open invoice for the reserve, so it gives back up to 1 cent more or less than its collection was charged (the review's case: +1,90 € charged, −1,91 € given back); the hold stays within ⌈(n + 1) / 1,2⌉ and is released at the file's next collection. The second review's claim (« a collection that lowered the commission gives back at most 1 cent less ») was wrong. The property now asserts |undo + charge| ≤ 1 when the undo issues a regularisation, exact otherwise. Netting the undo's regularisation against its own credit would remove the cent but contradicts « never netted across documents »: TO VALIDATE.
  - **A change of the VAT rate** (low). The cap and the reserve re-applied the new rate to what earlier invoices had charged at their own: after a rise from 10 % to 20 %, a 0,01 € collection credited 1,51 €. A run with no reversal on a key now never charges it less than before, except what its own collections take back by re-tiering (V ≥ charged before + min(0, the run's own change)); at an unchanged rate this never binds. A run with a reversal re-applies the cap at the new rate (TO VALIDATE). Golden `vat-rate-change`; property: with VAT rates changing between runs a run of collections never credits nor lowers V, and while the rate only rises the TTC billed stays within the basis. **Not covered (flagged):** after a fall of the rate, an invoice at the higher rate keeps its VAT and the TTC billed to a small file at the cap can exceed what it brought in (21,65 € for 20,01 € in a scratch case); the engine would need each open invoice's rate (a change of the M2c-4 contract), not built.
  - **For M2c-4**: runs are cut along the event order (the pending queue already takes items as posted); `compute` refuses a same-run reversal that does not mirror its collection; a paired line is persisted with commission 0 and no parts; nothing else changes in the contract.

# ADR-0013: Payments, allocation and client money (extends ADR-0012)

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-08
- Design: `docs/design/m2b-payments.md` (three-design panel and judge; legal texts re-read on LibreJustice). Founder answers on 2026-11-08: all 20 defaults accepted (see "Founder decisions" below).
- Implemented from M2b-1 (this ADR's ledger, bank-account, payment and reference parts) to M2b-6.

## Context
M2a booked what debtors owe (1100 receivable, 3100 owner claim). M2b books what they pay: money lands on the account dedicated to funds collected for creditors (CPCE R124-2), is matched to a claim, allocated in the legal order (C. civ. 1342-10, 1343-1), acknowledged by a receipt (R124-6) and reported to the client (R124-5). The ledger must keep client money provably separate from Collectoo's money (M2-12, CPCE-02) and never let a payment, an allocation or a receivable go below zero.

## Decision

### Names (design §4)
- Dedicated account: `bank_account.purpose = 'client_funds'` (ARCHITECTURE and COMPLIANCE wording; DOMAIN aligned). `'own'` arrives with M2c.
- Statement `bank_statement`; statement line `bank_transaction`; match record `payment_match` (M2b-2, M2b-3).
- Entry types: `cash_in`, `allocation`, `forward`, `cash_out`, `netting`.
- RF creditor reference (ISO 11649): `payment_reference`.

### Ledger extension (M2b-1)
- **Book 5120 `client_money_cash`**, dimension `bank`, money of the clients: one ledger account per bank account. A 5120 ledger account exists only for an **active `client_funds` bank account of the same servicer and currency** (guard, `LG002`; composite foreign key on organization and currency). 5121 PSP clearing (M5), 5122 own cash and 6270 fees (M2c) stay unseeded: refused (fail closed).
- **Unallocated money is pooled**: one 4710 ledger account per servicer and currency, no client. What belongs to which payment is told by `ledger_entry.payment_id`, not by the ledger account.
- **Entry types and their books** (`ledger_entry_type.allowed_books`): `cash_in` 5120/4710; `allocation` 4710/4670/1100/3100; `forward` 4710/4670; `cash_out` 4710/5120; `netting` 1100 only. **`adjustment` loses 4710 and 4670**: client money never moves through a generic correction. No payment type reaches 7060 (M2-12).
- **`ledger_entry.payment_id`** (FK to `payment`): required on `allocation`, `forward`, `cash_out`; allowed on their reversals, which must keep it; refused elsewhere. A `cash_in` names its bank statement and is **never reversed** (a wrong statement line is corrected by a payment-tagged `cash_out`).
- **Checks at commit, per entry** (`private.ledger_entry_verify`, key lookups only, `enable_seqscan = off`):
  - I2 closure: lines on client-money books (5120, 4710, 4670) sum to zero per currency (`LG001`). Only M2c `commission` will be exempt.
  - I3 shapes: `cash_in` = one 5120 debit + one pooled 4710 credit; `cash_out` = one pooled 4710 debit + one 5120 credit; `forward` = one pooled 4710 debit + one 4670 credit; `allocation` = one pooled 4710 debit, one 4670 credit, ≥ 1 receivable credits, no receivable debit, owner-claim debits; `netting` on principal only (`LG001`).
  - I4 payment: for every entry of a payment p, at every date t, `amount_p·[t ≥ booking date] − Σ 4710 debits of p's entries dated ≤ t ≥ 0`, and the net allocated per (payment, account) is never negative (**new SQLSTATE `LG007`**, Python `OverdrawnError`). An entry dated before the payment's booking date fails this way.
  - I6 receivables: a 1100 account credited by an `allocation` or a `netting` dated D never goes below zero from D on; a credit item debited by a `netting` never goes above zero from D on (`LG007`). **Checked for every entry that moves such an account, corrections and reversals included**, so the invariant holds after any sequence of entries, not only after allocations.
- **Allocation lock** (design §5.2): `pg_advisory_xact_lock(hashtext('payments:' || organization_id))`, taken by `ledger_post_set` for the types that lower a payment or a receivable, by `ledger_reverse`, and by `ledger_post` whenever a correction touches a receivable, up or down (lowering one can overdraw an allocated item; raising one can push a netted credit item above zero). With every such writer serialised, the commit-time checks of two sessions never miss each other's entries (read committed).
- **Writes:** `private.ledger_post` refuses the five payment types. They are posted only by **`private.ledger_post_set()`**, set-based, called from other SECURITY DEFINER functions (no grant): the caller runs `private.ledger_post_set_begin()` (drops and recreates `pg_temp._lps_entry` / `_lps_line`, ADR-0012 temp-schema rule), fills them, and calls it. Scope comes from the account, the payment and the bank account; idempotency key + canonical request hash per entry (`LG004`); it runs the commit-time checks before returning, so a refusal surfaces inside the caller's call.
- **Same migration, M2a functions redefined:** the pooled ledger-account unique index gains `bank_account_id`, so `ledger_post` and `post_opening_balances` get the new `ON CONFLICT` target (bodies otherwise unchanged; the M2a tests and the 50k confirmation run again).
- **Read access:** client-money ledger rows (portfolio-less 5120, 4710, 4670 and the entries without a portfolio) are read by admins and ops managers only (`private.money_staff_org_ids()`); clients read their own 4670. Collectors no longer see them (closes M2a risk #6). `public.ledger_balance` and `public.client_money_balances` apply the same rule.

### Bank accounts, payments, references (M2b-1)
- **`bank_account`**: servicer's accounts; IBAN checked per ISO 13616 (registry country, that country's exact length, mod 97; the same table in SQL, Python and TypeScript); purpose, IBAN, currency and opening date immutable; a closed account stays closed and a bank account is never deleted (ALWAYS triggers). Registered and closed by admins only (`register_bank_account`, `close_bank_account`, idempotency key + request hash); **closing requires a zero 5120 balance**, and is final: the page asks staff to retype the last 4 characters of the IBAN. A reversal never puts money back on a closed account (`ledger_reverse` checks the bank accounts of the lines it negates, `LG002`). An allocation lowers the receivables by exactly the money it moves (Σ1100 = −Σ4710, checked at commit). Read by admins and ops managers.
- **`payment`**: one row per debtor credit (later: per PSP event). Bank facts (amount, currency, dates, payer, references) immutable; never deleted; amount > 0; currency = the bank account's (composite FK). `account_id` null until matched, never a staged account; client and portfolio are copied from the account by a trigger and checked by a composite foreign key (`account_tenant_key`). The foreign key to `bank_transaction` is added in M2b-2. Read by admins and ops managers; collectors read the payments of their portfolios, never unmatched ones; clients read none (third-party payer data).
- **`payment_reference`**: RF + 2 check digits + **12 random digits** (no sequence: nothing about portfolio size leaks); valid mod 97; **one active reference per account** (partial unique index); retired references stay and keep resolving; only `active → retired` is allowed; never deleted. Issued set-based when a batch is confirmed (`private.issue_payment_references`, called by `complete_batch_confirmation`), back-filled by the migration for accounts confirmed before M2b. An admin can retire a reference (`retire_payment_reference`), which issues a new one. Readable by whoever reads the portfolio (clients print it on letters).
- **Check digits in SQL and Python:** `private.mod97 / rf_valid / rf_make / rf_normalise / iban_valid` and `collectoo_worker.payments.rf` give the same answer for every input (parity test on 10,000 random strings). Check digits outside 02–98 are refused (98 ≡ 1 mod 97 would otherwise give two spellings of one reference). Normalisation is ASCII-only on both sides (spaces and line breaks removed, a–z upper-cased) so that no locale changes a result.
- **Commands** record a request hash in `private.command_log`: the same key with another request is refused.

### Choices made where the design was open (M2b-1)
1. **I6 is enforced for every entry that moves an allocated or netted receivable**, not only for `allocation` and `netting` entries. The design states I6 as an invariant; checking only the allocation would let a later correction or reversal break it. Openings are exempt (claim books only, on new ledger accounts).
2. **Reversing a `cash_in` is refused** (`LG003`). The design corrects a wrong line with a payment-tagged `cash_out`; a reversed `cash_in` would break I11 (cash_in of a statement day = Σ its payments).
3. **Payment rows keep `bank_transaction_id` without its foreign key** until M2b-2 creates `bank_transaction` (as asked for this slice); M2b-2 adds the key and makes it required for the `camt053` and `csv` channels.
4. **`ledger_post_set` accepts one client per entry from the account, or from `client_organization_id` for a `forward`** (the only payment entry with a client and no account). An `account_id` is required on `allocation` and `netting` and refused on the others.
5. **The reference check in the table is written out inline** (the ISO formula for a digits-only body) and issuance draws the body inline: a function call per row cost 6.3 s for 50k references; inline, 2.6 s. A pgTAP test shows the inline check says what `rf_valid` says.
6. **The seed** issues references for its accounts (`select private.issue_payment_references(null)` at the end of `supabase/seed.sql`), as production does for accounts confirmed before M2b.

### Bank statements and the import (M2b-2, design §6–§9; migration `20261109120000_bank_statements.sql`)
- **`bank_statement`** (one row per file) moves `awaiting_upload → uploaded → imported | rejected | failed`; the import is one worker transaction, so « importing » is never stored. Bank account and file are fixed; a finished statement never changes; statements are never deleted. The same file (SHA-256) for the same bank account is **one statement**: the upload command returns it instead of creating a second one (a rejected or failed file can be sent again).
- **`bank_transaction`** (one row per line, append-only facts, no change log like the ledger) is deduplicated per bank account by the design's keys (`t:` line reference when every detail of the entry has one, `e:` entry reference + index from 1, `c:` CSV bank id, `h:` fingerprint + occurrence). An entry kept as one line is `e:<ref>#1`, with or without a single detail, so the same entry keeps its key whatever detail a file gives. A known key **with another amount, direction or booking date refuses the whole file** (`reference_conflict`): a line is never dropped because a reference was reused. A key twice in one file refuses it too. Imports of one bank account are serialised (advisory lock).
- **One bank entry, one booking (review fix):** every camt.053 line keeps its entry's own reference (`entry_ref` = `Ntry/AcctSvcrRef`, indexed per bank account). A new line of an entry the account already has means the entry came back split another way (unsplit then detailed, or the reverse): the file is refused (`entry_detail_differs`), since its money would be booked twice. Entries without a reference have only the `h:` fingerprint and no such check.
- **One file, one bank account (review fix):** a file (SHA-256) already sent or imported for another bank account of the servicer is refused at upload, and again at import under a lock (`imported_on_other_account`). A CSV line with the bank id of a line on another account of the servicer, same day, amount and direction, refuses its file the same way; a CSV line whose fingerprint (`h:`) is on another account is held as a suspected duplicate. The lookups behind these checks are index probes per new line whatever the statistics say.
- **A payment is its bank line:** composite foreign key (line, servicer, bank account, currency, amount, booking date), required for the `camt053` and `csv` channels; one payment per line (M2b-1's unique key). So I11 (cash_in of a statement day = Σ its payments) is checked from the tables, in the import's transaction (`LG001` otherwise).
- **Booked at import:** every credit a debtor paid becomes a `payment` (`unmatched`) and each booking day of the statement one `cash_in` entry (`cash_in:<statement>:<date>`, Dr 5120 / Cr 4710 pool) through `ledger_post_set`. Debits (fees, our refunds, returns), credits that undo a debit, and held lines are recorded, **not booked** (M2b-6, decision #20); I9 is computed at import (`closing = 5120 + lines not booked`) and an unexplained difference is shown, not refused (it means a missing statement or money on the account before the first import).
- **Readers:** camt.053 .001.02 to .001.13 by local name; CSV with the documented column set (DOMAIN §3.5). Amounts are exact decimals refused, never rounded, beyond the currency's exponent (zeros excepted). XML: a document type declaration is refused before parsing, lxml runs with entities, DTD loading and network off and its default size limits, streaming with constant memory (111 MB peak for 100k lines); a statement of more than 250,000 lines is refused while it streams (`file_too_large`, the CSV reader's cap). Dates are `xs:date` as written: a time zone suffix (`2026-10-01+01:00`, `Z`) is accepted and not applied.

### Choices made where the design was open (M2b-2)
1. **An unsplit batch credit is one payment, booked at import.** The design queues `batch_unsplit` for ops to split; since a bank line is at most one payment (unique key), « split » can only mean allocating that one payment across files (M2b-3 `assign_payment` splits). Booking it at once follows decision #1 and keeps the ledger cash equal to the bank. The line keeps kind `batch_unsplit` so the queue shows it, unless it undoes an earlier movement (choice #2 wins). A batch is unsplit when its details do not add up, carry no amount, another currency or direction, or their count differs from `NbOfTxs`; a single detail always takes the booked entry amount.
2. **A credit that undoes a debit** (reversal indicator, return reason, return sub-family) is a `return` line, not a payment: not booked until M2b-6 links it. This holds for a batch that cannot be split too: the entry's reversal indicator or sub-family, or a return reason or sub-family in any of its details, makes the whole line a `return` (the first reason is kept).
3. **One statement format per bank account** (`bank_account.feed`): CAMT and CSV lines carry different dedup keys, so mixing them could book the same money twice. The upload command refuses the other format.
4. **CSV bank id only under an unambiguous header** (« Référence opération », « Référence banque »…). A plain « Référence » column may hold the payer's reference, which repeats and would hide a payment as already imported.
5. **CSV suspected duplicates** (design §8.3 step 5) are held with no payment and no cash, except when both lines carry the bank's own id (then the bank says they differ). Releasing a held line (`resolve_duplicate`) comes with the M2b-3 queue; the import adds a second `cash_in` for that day then, so I11 is read per (statement, date) over all its `cash_in` entries.
6. **Pending/information entries and zero amounts** are skipped and counted; lines booked after today are refused (the ledger refuses future dates).
7. The `match` and `process_debits` jobs of design §8.3 step 7 are not queued yet (M2b-3, M2b-6).
8. **The CSV reader refuses what it cannot read one way only** (M2b-2 review; DOMAIN §3.5). A CSV `return` comes only from the bank's operation type at the start of the label (« RETOUR VIR SEPA », « PRLV SEPA REJETÉ »), never from words the payer may have written: a credit wrongly read as a payment stays visible in matching, a payment wrongly read as a return would never be booked. The encoding is decided over the whole file (UTF-8 with or without BOM, else Windows-1252; anything else `csv_encoding`). A field over a line break (an unclosed quote), a row without its date, label or amount cells, or with non-empty cells beyond the header, and a negative `Crédit` are refused (`csv_row`). When `Date de comptabilisation` (or `Date comptable`) and `Date opération` (or `Date`) are both present, the first is the booking date (BPCE exports); two of one rank are refused. A CSV has at most 250,000 lines and 64 MiB (`file_too_large`, before parsing: the worker holds the parsed lines, about 0.7 KB each, in the process that also runs boarding). « ligne N » and a CSV line's `line_no` are the file's line, header and preamble included. An unexpected parser error is a refusal (`parse_failed`): the file would fail the same way on every retry.
9. **« Relevé manquant » is checked against the statement that covers the day before** (review fix): of the imported statements that start before the new one, the one that ends last. If it ends on or after the new statement's first day, the day before is covered already (only the overlap is reported); only a statement's closing at its last day is known, so an older statement is never compared.

### Reconciliation (M2b-3, design §6–§9, §12.2; migration `20261109150000_payment_matching.sql`)
- **Rules in the worker, decisions in SQL.** The rules R1–R6 are deterministic Python (`collectoo_worker.payments.matching`, `RULES` with the French title and explanation ops read), version `match-1` recorded on every decision. The job `payments.match` (queued by the import, by a batch confirmation and by `unassign_payment`) locks the waiting payments, loads only what their signals point to (references, invoice and file numbers, IBANs, then those files) through analysed temp tables, runs the rules and COPYs the decisions to `private.apply_match_results`, which **gates** each automatic match (design §8.4: file not confirmed, closed, other currency, before servicing start, surendettement / collective-proceeding hold, nothing left to pay) and records it. No model, no LLM: the same code runs in the eval.
- **`payment_match`** keeps every decision (append-only): `assign` by a rule (R1–R4, no person) or by ops (`manual`, with who and why), and `unassign` (ops). **`payment_match_candidate`** holds the files proposed for a payment left to ops (up to 3, ranked, with their signals), replaced at each run; only a single best suggestion of a confirmed open file is « one tap » (`acceptable`). **`private.payer_account_link`** is the IBAN memory (never exposed).
- **Commands** (admins and ops managers at aal2, idempotency key + request hash): `assign_payment` (one file, optional designation of its invoices, a reason), `accept_suggestions` (≤ 200 payments of one servicer, each with the file the page showed: compare and set; the others are skipped and said so on the page), `unassign_payment` (nothing allocated yet; back to the queue), `place_account_hold` / `lift_account_hold`. Reads: `payment_queue`, `match_queue_accounts`, `search_payment_files`, `statement_match_counts`. Page « Paiements à traiter » (`/backoffice/encaissements/a-traiter`).

### Choices made where the design was open (M2b-3)
1. **Two files named is never automatic.** Beyond the design's « two valid references → ambiguous » and « same number in several files → ambiguous »: every cited number must name the same single file (intersection), and an RF of file A together with a number that names only file B is `ambiguous` too (R1/R2 would otherwise match A while the debtor may be paying both; ops choose).
2. **A reference whose check digits fail is never guessed from:** its digits are not read again as a bare 12-digit body, and it stops every automatic match of the payment (review fix 1 below). In free text, every length whose check holds after « RF » is looked up (a longer prefix passes mod 97 one time in 97).
3. **R3 covers files not confirmed yet** (« workable » read as « not closed »), so the gating can say `account_not_confirmed` and the rematch at confirmation matches them (founder #11; such files have no RF and no balance yet: backing by name or IBAN). **R3 on a closed file is only a suggestion**; R1/R2 on a closed file match it and gate it `account_closed` (a recalled file included: the payment table has no « recalled » reason; the queue shows the file's status).
4. **IBAN memory.** Taught by R1, R2, R3 and ops decisions; an R4 match only refreshes it (no self-reinforcement). An unassignment forgets what its assignment taught (count − 1). R4 needs one open file linked by at least one such decision and no other file linked (whatever its status, the run's own matches included: review fix 4 below), and the amount within what is left to pay counting the run's earlier matches.
5. **Ops decisions win.** A payment ops assigned or unassigned gets only suggestions from the rules afterwards, never a file it was taken off.
6. **« Left to pay »** (gating `nothing_due`, R4) = Σ receivable − payments matched and not allocated − earlier payments of the same batch, until M2b-4 tracks U per payment. Payments are gated again when a hold is placed or lifted and when a payment leaves the file.
7. **One file per payment.** `assign_payment` takes `splits[]` (the design's signature) with one element: splitting a payment across files comes with allocation (M2b-4), where the amounts matter. For an ambiguous payment ops choose one file.
8. **Rematch at confirmation:** the confirmation job queues `match` (`trigger: rematch`) for every payment left to ops of the servicer (`no_match`, `ambiguous`, `account_not_confirmed`, never tried). A rolled-back file leaves the payments that waited for it `no_mandate` (statement trigger on account delete; refund or ratification, 1342-2). Decisions keep the rule's own method; `trigger` says `rematch` (the `rematch` method value is not used).
9. **R6 name hints** come from a word index of the servicer's party names built in the job (a GIN trigram search per payer took 7–110 ms: minutes for thousands of payers); the GIN index serves the ops search (`search_payment_files`).
10. **IBAN shape only in SQL:** the memory stores the IBAN as the worker reads it; a mod-97 check per row cost 7.7 s at 50k (the worker checks the digits before any lookup).
11. **Minimal `account_hold`:** kinds `dispute`, `surendettement`, `collective_proceeding`, `recalled`, `wrong_party`; scope set by kind (DOMAIN §4); evidence (a document of the file) required for surendettement and collective proceedings; only the end is ever written; no UI yet (file page, M2b-4). The `payment_held` client notification needs `client_notification` (M2b-4).
12. **Commands wait at most 2 s** for a payment the match job holds (`lock_timeout`), then ask to retry.
13. **Deferred:** releasing a CSV suspected duplicate (`resolve_duplicate`, announced for M2b-3 in M2b-2 choice #5) goes to M2b-6 with `link_debit`: both are decisions on bank lines, not on payments.

### Review fixes to the rules and the eval (M2b-3 review, 2026-11-09)
Rule version stays `match-1` (no decision was recorded with the earlier rules outside tests).
1. **An RF the payer wrote that names none of our files blocks every automatic match** (review #0, #3). A structured reference starting with « RF » whose check fails, a valid RF that is not one of the servicer's references (another creditor's), or an RF in the text none of whose readings is ours: the rule that would have matched — R1/R2 on another, valid RF, R3, or R4 — gives a suggestion instead, never one tap, with `why: unresolved_reference` and the reference as written in the candidate's signals (only candidate signals reach the queue). Alone, the payment goes to R5/R6, the reference in the evidence. After a failed « RF », at least the issued length is never read again as a bare body, even when a shorter reading passes mod 97 by chance.
2. **A date never names a file** (review #1). Keys of digits only shaped YYYYMM, MMYYYY, YYYYMMDD or DDMMYYYY (years 19xx, 20xx) are not looked up across files (`is_lookup_token`, so the job never queries them); they still designate the matched file's invoices. The exact amount backs R3 only when a cited number has letters and a number that is not a date (« FA-2026-00042 », not « 7730219 » or « LOYER 2026-11 »); otherwise R3 needs the payer's IBAN or name. The amount is compared with the invoices the payer designates in that file.
3. **The payer's name backs a number when it shares two words with a party, or one word no other file has** (review #2, #5): similarity ≥ 0.6 as before, and two words shared with one party (a word of 5 letters or more may carry one typo), or one word written exactly that no other file of the servicer has (`RARE_NAME_FILES = 1`, counted in the servicer's name index): otherwise the debtor of that other file, mistyping a number, could be the payer. Surname particles (LE, LA, DE, DU, DES) are name words; initials must agree with the other name's words (« J DUPONT » is not « Marie Dupont »; a single-word name constrains nothing; d', l' are elisions; a legal form spelt « S.A.R.L. » is dropped); ligatures are written out (Œ → OE, Æ → AE, ß → SS; `ref_key` is unchanged, for its SQL twin). The job builds the name index after R4, with the remainder: an R3 decision resting on one word waits for it, and R4 on its IBAN or its file waits too; `remainder` also receives those payments. A unit test runs the eval's statement in the job's loading order and gets the same decisions.
   - Measured on the eval's 50k world (Faker fr_FR: 400 surnames, so every name word is on at least 126 files), 20,000 payments citing the adjacent FA-2026 number of another file, with the debtor's name as banks print it: **15 wrong automatic matches before, 0 after**; on the new generator's world, which adds such neighbours (« Guillot » next to « Guillot et Fils »): 126 before, 0 after.
   - **Residual risk, accepted with founder decision #3:** two files whose parties share the same two-word name (true homonyms, or one debtor with two files of the same client): a mistyped invoice number from one is matched to the other; nothing in the payment tells them apart. Every match stays reversible (unassign).
   - **Cost:** a company known by one surname that other files also carry no longer backs a number (a one-tap suggestion instead). In the eval's world that is 70 % of the companies: with the earlier generator, 44 % of the name-backed invoice credits went to the queue (auto 95.5 % → 89.0 %, 0 wrong). Real portfolios have far fewer shared surnames; the real files of decision #19 will tell. The generator now draws the debtors of those credits among names that back a number, as it already drew only unique numbers.
4. **IBAN memory: every file the IBAN paid, whatever its status, the run's own matches included** (review #4, #11). « This file and only this one » counts every file linked by at least one decision — a closed, recalled or paid file too (listed as a candidate, so ops can forward) — so a rematch never flips a payment to the other file once one closes. A run decides R1–R3 for all its payments first, then R4, which counts the links the run's R1–R3 matches teach wherever they sit in the statement: an IBAN that pays file B by its RF is no longer « only A » for its other credits of that statement (property test over every order). An IBAN that paid only a closed or unconfirmed file gives R4 nothing (R5/R6 decide).
5. **The eval fails on a policy violation** (review #20): a credit whose truth is the queue (name and amount only, a mistyped or unknown reference, two files) matched automatically, even to the right file, counts in `policy_violations`, which must be 0 like `wrong` (eval, unit test, both integration tests). The generator writes the hard cases: an IBAN that paid A pays B by RF and sends a bare credit in the same statement; a debtor cites a month only another lease at the same rent has (from a new IBAN: queue; from his own IBAN: R4); mistyped RFs from an IBAN that paid one file; two RFs one of which is mistyped; the next invoice number of a file whose name shares one common word with the payer's; names with Œ/Æ. They take 0.7 % from structured RF and share the 4 % unmatchable with the earlier cases.

### Review fixes (M2b-3, before merge; the migration was edited in place: not yet applied in production)
14. **Accepting a suggestion is compare and set.** `accept_suggestions(key, items)` takes, per payment, the file the page showed (`[{payment_id, account_id}]`, both in the request hash) and assigns it only if that file is still the payment's one-tap suggestion, under the payment's lock (a rematch may replace candidates between the page and the tap; a decision ops never saw must not be recorded as theirs, nor teach the IBAN memory). Otherwise the payment is skipped: `suggestion_changed`, `no_suggestion`, `not_waiting`; the page says « N suggestions acceptées ; M paiements ignorés : suggestion modifiée entre-temps (k)… ».
15. **Holds and rollbacks are serialised with the gating, on the file.** The gating first locks the run's files `FOR KEY SHARE` in id order; `place_account_hold` and `lift_account_hold` lock the file `FOR UPDATE` (the file before the hold). A hold placed or lifted during a run has either committed (the gating sees it) or waits for the run (its regate then sees the run's payments). Not a per-servicer advisory lock: every hold command would then wait for the whole apply of any run of the servicer (≈ 10 s at 50k, beyond the commands' 2 s `lock_timeout`); the row lock only meets runs that touch that file. `KEY SHARE`, not `SHARE`: a batch confirmation updating a file's status does not wait. A file gone by then (its batch rolled back during the run, also between the rules and the apply) leaves the payment `no_mandate`, its file kept as candidate, as the delete trigger does: never « waiting » for a deleted file, never « not tried » for ever.
16. **Bounded runs.** One match job takes at most 50,000 payments (`MATCH_CHUNK`) in processing order (booking date, statement, line) and, when the chunk is full, queues its continuation (`after`: where it ended) in its own transaction: a redelivered job replays the same chunk; a backlog is a chain of bounded transactions (a rematch too: its targets stay targets, the cursor moves on).
17. **Queue reads.** `payment_queue` runs one query per tab, each on a partial index whose predicate is the tab's, led by the servicer, for admins and ops managers (`money_staff_org_ids`; collectors never had the page): a page reads the queue, not the payment history, with or without fresh statistics. A page past the last one shows the real total and a link to the last page.
18. **The evidence of a designation** (`source = document`) is a document filed on the chosen file, not any document of the client.
19. **Payments imported before this migration** (M2b-2 is live) get one match job per servicer at migration time (`private.queue_waiting_matches`).
20. **The confirmation sheet states the gating's outcome** before anything is sent (`match_queue_accounts` returns the servicing start and the active blocking holds): « Il sera imputé… » only when the payment will be ready; otherwise « sera mis en attente » with the reason (closed, other currency, before servicing start, hold, nothing left to pay).
21. **Plans that rest on no fresh statistics.** Anti-joins, never `NOT IN (subquery)` (above about 200k rows it is a plain subplan, one scan of the subquery per row: 250k × 4k rows, 40 s against 25 ms for `NOT EXISTS`). The gating's file table is keyed and analysed, filled by one aggregate per fact, every join on an indexed key: one statement of left joins over the aggregates was planned, after a 50k import, as a nested loop over an aggregate estimated at one row (apply 44 s instead of 10 s).

### Allocation (M2b-4, design §6–§9, §12; migration `20261109190000_payment_allocation.sql`)
- **Waterfall in the worker, posting in SQL.** `collectoo_worker.payments.allocation` (engine `alloc-1`) is a pure function: amount, open slots at the date, policy, designation → lines in minor units and the remainder. Order (founder #4, #5): designated items first (even not yet due, 1305-3; among them the legal order below), then due items by due date then issue date (equal keys pro rata, largest remainder, ties by item id), then items not yet due; inside an item interest, accessories where the policy ranks them, principal, the seller's unclassified amounts (held for consumer regimes, founder #6). A slot's availability at D is the lowest running balance from D on (design §2 #6); an item's payable slots are capped at the item's net availability (all its components, held and negative ones included), the excess staying an overpayment (review fixes below). Hypothesis properties in `worker/tests/test_allocation.py`.
- **Job `payments.allocate`** (queued by a statement trigger on `payment` whenever a payment becomes ready): the servicer's allocation lock, at most `ALLOCATION_CHUNK` (10,000) ready payments in processing order with a queued continuation, the files locked FOR KEY SHARE before the payments FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED (a hold command locks the file first: no deadlock), the files' receivables loaded as per-date profiles, plans computed in order on in-memory balances, COPY, then `private.post_allocations`: set-based re-checks (payment still ready on that file, gates, lines on the file's items, Σ lines ≤ what is left of the payment → `LG007`, no principal while the item's interest is available at the date → `LG006`), one `allocation` entry per (payment, file) through `ledger_post_set` (key `alloc:<run>:<payment>:<file>`, `engine_version`, `inputs_hash`), the plan header (`private.allocation_plan`: policy, designation, inputs hash), one `client_notification` per entry, the payment's state, the run's summary (`allocation_run`).
- **States** (design §7): `allocated` (nothing left), `partially_allocated` + `overpayment` (the rest stays in 4710 for the payment: never forwarded, founder #8), or still `matched` with `nothing_due`, `credit_note_to_apply` or a gate.
- **Commands** (admins and ops managers at aal2, idempotency key + request hash, each comparing with what the page showed): `reverse_allocation` (the live allocation entries of one file, by id; reversed at their dates; the client told), `allocate_payment` (« Imputer »: from `allocation_reversed`, `nothing_due`, `credit_note_to_apply`, `before_servicing_start`; or what is left of an overpayment for another file of the same debtor on his written request), `release_hold_payment` (judge's authorisation filed on the file; the hold stays), `forward_payment` (what is left of a payment nothing was allocated from: recalled file, before the cut-off, or a rolled-back batch's payment on the client's ratification), `apply_credit_note` (the proposal's key), `set_allocation_policy` (admins). Reads: `payment_detail`, `account_detail`. Pages « Paiement » (`/backoffice/encaissements/paiements/[id]`) and « Dossier » (`/backoffice/dossiers/[id]`).

### Review fixes to the SQL, the job and the pages (M2b-4 review, before merge; the migration was edited in place: not yet applied in production)
1. **An item is never collected beyond what it owes as a whole** (backstop of the waterfall's cap). `post_allocations` refuses (`LG007`) an allocation that would take an item's receivable — every component summed, held and negative ones included (a negative balancing line: components above 3.12) — below zero at any date from the allocation's on, this run's earlier allocations on the item counted in date order; the same definition as the waterfall's per-item cap, so a valid plan is never refused. One keyed lateral lookup per (payment, item): no measurable cost (50k allocations 53.3 s before, 50.8 s after, same session; `docs/benchmarks/payments.md`).
2. **Holds come before the cut-off**, in the match gating (`gate_assignments`, redefined), the job (`gate`) and the confirmation sheet: a payment booked before the servicing start on a held file is reported held, so the client gets its `payment_held` notification (founder #12, #17).
3. **Ops' releases add up.** `allocation_instruction` holds a set of gates released (`releases`) and every decision (`decisions`: who, when, why, the evidence); « Imputer » and « Lever le blocage » add to it; the job, the backstop and the gating pass every released gate; the job keeps the instruction when a payment stops at another gate of its own file and consumes it when it allocates (or when the payment comes back from another file). A payment behind two gates (hold and cut-off, hold and credit note, cut-off and credit note) used to alternate between them for ever.
4. **A held file's payments are never forwarded**, and **compensation is not invoked on a held file**: `forward_payment` and `apply_credit_note` refuse while a surendettement or collective-proceeding hold is active (L722-5; L622-7 I allows only the compensation of connected claims, which the code cannot tell; OQ-23, OQ-24). Such a payment is refunded (M2b-6) or allocated on the judge's authorisation; the credit note is shown, not applied. Both commands lock the file `FOR KEY SHARE` (a hold placed meanwhile is serialised with them).
5. **Gating again is compare and set.** `regate_account` writes only a payment still `matched` to that file and not sent to another file (READ COMMITTED re-checks these on the row the allocation job committed): it never stamps a gate on a payment the job allocated meanwhile, nor gates a payment sent to another file on its own file's facts.
6. **The client reads codes, never ops' motif.** `client_notification.reason` is a code (`hold_blocks_allocation`, `ops_correction`, `file_recalled`, `before_servicing_start`, `client_ratification`); reversal and forward entries carry a fixed French wording; the motif is kept for staff in `private.payment_ops_note` and shown on the payment page (« Motif interne »).
7. **Reversing the payment's own file while another file keeps its share** leaves it `partially_allocated` + `allocation_reversed` (that file may owe it again), not an overpayment; « Imputer » sends it back to its file.
8. **The file page's payments** are two indexed lookups (the file's payments, and those with an entry on it), never an OR over a subquery (a scan of every payment of every servicer: 36.6 ms at 50k payments, 23.6 ms with a parallel scan at 164k; 0.5 and 0.1 ms now); collectors see only payments of portfolios they read.
9. **Pages say what is true:** notifications are « enregistrée pour le client (envoi : à venir) » until delivered (no claim that the client was informed); a forwarded payment reads « Reversé au client », never « Imputé »; an allocation row on another file names that file; the order note describes what the engine applies, citing 1342-10 and 1343-1 only for what they say; a stale « Désimputer » says the allocation was already reversed.

### Choices made where the design was open (M2b-4)
1. **Queued by a trigger.** A statement-level trigger on `payment` (fires ALWAYS) queues one allocation job per servicer whenever a payment becomes ready, and writes the `payment_held` notification when a payment becomes held: every path (a match, ops' assignment or acceptance, a hold lifted, an ops decision) is covered without each command knowing.
2. **Single-payment commands post at once** (reversal, forward, credit note) under the allocation lock with `lock_timeout = 2s` (« réessayez » while an allocation runs), instead of « RPCs only enqueue » (design §2 #15): they take milliseconds and ops see the result. Decisions that need the waterfall (allocate again, release a hold, overpayment to another file) are recorded on the payment (`allocation_instruction`: the gate ops let it pass, or the other file and the evidence) and the job allocates; the instruction is void once the payment leaves its file, and the gating run on hold changes (`regate_account`, redefined) honours it and leaves the M2b-4 reasons alone.
3. **A reversed payment waits** (`allocation_reversed`, a new review reason): otherwise the job would allocate it again the same way. Ops then unassign it or send it to allocation again.
4. **Credit notes hold the file's payments.** Compensation operates « à la date où ses conditions se trouvent réunies » once invoked (1347): allocating first could pay the very invoice the credit note compensates. Proposal per file: the oldest credit against the first group of due invoices in the imputation order (1347-1 « exigibles », 1347-4 transposes the imputation rules), pro rata on ties, dated when both were due, not before servicing start, principal only; one proposal at a time. Payments wait (`credit_note_to_apply`) until ops apply it, or allocate without it (compensation not invoked).
5. **Forward** only when nothing was allocated from the payment, dated the action day; the payment is then `allocated` (design §7: A counts forwards). A rolled-back batch's payment needs a document of the ratifying client (OQ-29); the page offers forward for recalled files and payments before the cut-off (the no-mandate case is the SQL command only for now).
6. **Overpayment on another file** of the same debtor: same client and a shared borrower or co-borrower party (debtors are not merged across clients), the debtor's written request filed on either file.
7. **Policy versions are never retroactive** (`effective_from` ≥ today); in a scope, the version that took effect last wins, then the higher version; no row: the built-in default (accessories before principal, CALCULATION_RULES §6; unclassified last). Consumer regimes (`consumer_credit_pre_ccd2`, `consumer_credit_ccd2`, `consumer_mortgage`) force `held`.
8. **Items without a due date** count as due, after the dated ones; an unknown issue date sorts last.
9. **Plans** are temp tables; only a header per posted entry is kept (`private.allocation_plan`): the lines are the entry's postings.
10. **`client_notification` has no foreign keys** (as `ledger_posting`): a set-based ALWAYS guard checks payment, entry, client, file and kind, with hash and merge joins off (key lookups whatever the statistics); the referenced rows are never deleted once they carry money.
11. **« Rapprochés »** (`payment_queue`, redefined) lists every payment with a file and nothing to decide, allocated ones included; a wrong one is reversed and unassigned from its page.
12. **Not in this slice:** the policy settings page (`/backoffice/parametres/imputation`; the command exists), receipts and the client feed (M2b-5), refunds, returns and direct payments (M2b-6). The ledger property machine is not extended to the waterfall (pure properties + integration tests cover it).

### Review fixes to the waterfall (M2b-4 review, before merge)
1. **An item is never paid beyond its net.** An EU loan's components add up to the legal balance 3.12 through a signed balancing line (founder 2026-11-07 #6). One slot per component ignored a negative one, so a payment above 3.12 paid principal and interest in full and the excess reached the client's 4670, against founder #8 and C. civ. 1302-1 (likewise a positive balancing line beside a negative principal). The waterfall now caps each item's payable slots at the item's net availability at D: min over t ≥ D of all its components summed, held and negative ones included (`FileBalances.item_available`, `cap_items`). The excess is trimmed from the item's lowest ranks first (interest last, 1343-1) and stays the payment's remainder (`overpayment`). Netting inside one item is not compensation (one debt); between items it stays an ops action (1347, choice 4). The plan's slots, and so its inputs hash, are the capped ones. The posting gets the matching set-based backstop (SQL side of the same fix).
2. **Designated items follow the legal order among themselves.** Design §8.5 said "in designation order", but no stored order is the debtor's: `assign_payment` stores uuid order, matching stores the file's order. A designation names debts, not an order or amounts per debt, so 1342-10 decides among them: due before not due, oldest due date first, equal keys pro rata (« toutes choses égales, elle se fait proportionnellement »). The designation is hashed as a set. This departs from design §8.5 (founder note).

### Receipts and the client feed (M2b-5, design §6, §9, §10, §12, §13; migration `20261110090000_receipts_feed.sql`)
- **`receipt`** (append-only, RLS): **one number per payment linked to a file** (`Q-AAAA-NNNNNN`, a counter per servicer and year of issue, not gapless), **versions** `original` (v1) → `update` | `void`; current = highest version. A version is issued only when the payload differs from the current one (`payload_sha256` = SHA-256 of the canonical jsonb text): the job is idempotent. Each version has its `document` (private bucket `documents`, `receipts/{servicer}/{year}/{number}-v{version}.pdf`, SHA-256), its recipient (the file's borrower, R124-6 « remise au débiteur »), `template_version`, `renderer_version`, `known_at` (the job's start). A guard checks each new version follows the previous one of its payment (same number, no gap), names the payment's current file (a void one: the file it cancels), and that its document is its PDF.
- **Payload built in SQL from the ledger** (`private.receipt_payload`): issuer « agissant pour le compte de » the client, file reference, debtor, payer, the payment as the bank reported it (amount, currency, method, booking and value dates, end-to-end reference), the allocation per item and component (net of reversals, from the postings), totals allocated / forwarded / returned / not allocated (text, integer minor units; they add up to the payment), the first-letter reference (empty until M4). **No remaining balance** (founder #14), no review reason: a held payment reads « Montant non imputé » (neutral words).
- **Payer (founder #15):** named, as the bank wrote it, only when the words of his name (upper case, accents and ligatures written out, titles, legal forms, joining words and initials dropped: the matching rules' list) are exactly the words of a party to the file; otherwise « Versement d'un tiers », and the payload does not hold the name at all (GDPR art. 5(1)(c)). A joint account naming someone else, or an initial, is a third party.
- **Rendering (worker, `payments.receipt_pdf`):** reportlab **4.4.10** (pinned in `uv.lock`), invariant mode, **no compression** (the bytes do not depend on the zlib build), DejaVu Sans 2.37 committed with its licence (`worker/src/collectoo_worker/payments/fonts/`), the PDF time stamp and document id seeded from `known_at` (never the clock, nor `SOURCE_DATE_EPOCH`). PDF = f(row, payload, template `quittance-fr-1`, renderer `reportlab-4.4.10/dejavu-2.37/r1`). Golden SHA-256 of a fixed synthetic receipt in `tests/test_receipts.py`; the integration test renders a stored receipt again in another process and gets its bytes. French template locked (`payments.receipts`): amounts « 1 234,56 € », dates « 09/11/2026 »; no LLM, no mention the law does not require (OQ-22 open).
- **Job `payments.receipts`** (200 payments per message; a longer list is cut and the rest queued): the servicer's receipts lock (not the allocation lock: receipts never hold up allocations or ops), `receipt_prepare` (payloads, versions due, numbers drawn), render and upload (8 uploads at a time), `receipt_issue` (documents and receipts). **Queued** by a statement trigger on `ledger_entry` (every payment-tagged entry: allocation, forward, reversal; cash_out in M2b-6) and one on `payment` (a file assigned or taken off, a gate stamped on the same status), never for a payment ready for allocation (its allocation queues it); **backfilled** by the migration (`private.queue_receipt_backfill`) for the payments matched before it.
- **`client_payment_feed(p_client, p_after, p_limit)`**: the client's `client_notification` rows, newest first (cursor = created_at|id, index `(client, created_at desc, id desc)`), amounts from the entry's 4670 posting (a held payment: its amount), the items an allocation or its reversal credited, the receipt the client last had (latest version on its own file). Client members (their readable portfolios) and the servicer's admins and ops managers; **a client member reading rows records their delivery** (`delivered_at`, once). Page « Paiements reçus » (`/client/paiements`); receipt PDFs at `/client/quittances/[id]` and `/backoffice/quittances/[id]`, read with the user's session (RLS on `receipt` and `document`, storage policy `receipt_object_read`) and checked against the recorded SHA-256 before they are served.
- **Read access** (design §6, founder #18): receipts and their PDF by money staff and whoever reads the portfolio (clients their own, collectors their portfolios).

### Choices made where the design was open (M2b-5)
1. **Which payments get a receipt:** every payment with a file that is not waiting for its allocation (allocated, partially allocated, held or otherwise gated, forwarded); not one ready for allocation (its allocation follows within the job's run and queues the receipt: no throw-away version « nothing allocated yet »), not one without a file (no known debtor, design §2 #8).
2. **A payment taken off its file gets a `void` version** about the file it cancels (the earlier version named the wrong debtor); assigned again, it gets an `update` version with the new file, client and debtor, same number (design §8.2 variant A). `void` is final only for a bank return (M2b-6).
3. **The receipt and the feed show the client the receipt of its own file only:** after a payment moved to another client's file, the first client keeps its void version; the new client sees the new one.
4. **The issue date printed is `known_at`'s Paris date; no address of the issuer** (organizations have none yet: the design's « name and address » gets the address when it is modelled), no RF reference (not in the design's list).
5. **Delivery of a notification = read by a client member in the client space** (portal delivery; webhooks are M7); staff reading the feed deliver nothing. The payment page says « remise à … dans son espace client le … ».
6. **No compression, larger files:** ~87 KB per receipt (two font subsets of ~36 KB each), ~4.3 GB per 50,000 receipts; compression or a lighter font subset can come with a zlib-independent determinism check.
7. **Receipts of one servicer are issued one job at a time** (one number counter; a payment's versions in order; a failed attempt's upload at a version's key is replaced by its retry, which renders the same bytes). Parallelism is across servicers. Measured: ~11 ms a receipt (render ~9 ms, uploads alongside), 50k in ~9–10 min (`docs/benchmarks/payments.md`), within design risk R8.
8. **Sending receipts to debtors** (email, post, portal) stays out of scope: generated and stored only (design §10 go-live gate).

## Founder decisions (2026-11-08, all defaults accepted)
1. Money on the dedicated account is booked as soon as the statement is imported, in a "to be allocated" account, before we know whose file it is.
2. Each file gets one payment reference (RF + 14 digits, printed in groups of four) when its batch is confirmed; files already confirmed get one now.
3. Automatic matching only on a valid RF reference, on an invoice or file number backed by the payer's name, a known IBAN or the exact amount, or on an IBAN that already paid this file and only this one; name plus amount is only a suggestion.
4. A payment pays the invoices the debtor names, then due invoices oldest first (same date: pro rata), then invoices not yet due; inside each invoice interest, then principal, then the seller's unclassified amounts.
5. Clients and portfolios cannot change "interest before principal" or the legal order; they only set where future fees and indemnities rank.
6. Consumer loans: the seller's unclassified amounts are not paid by allocation until ops classify them; the extra is an overpayment.
7. Credit notes are proposed automatically and applied with one ops tap (compensation must be invoked).
8. An overpayment stays with us, is never passed to the client, and is refunded to the payer by ops (or used for another file of the same debtor on written request).
9. A payment in another currency than the debt is not converted; it goes to ops and is refunded by default.
10. A payment dated before the cut-off goes to ops: passed to the client if the seller's balance already counts it, otherwise allocated at the cut-off date.
11. A payment on a file not yet confirmed waits and is matched again at confirmation; if the batch is rolled back, it is refunded unless the client ratifies it (confirmed after an explanation).
12. Surendettement (admissible) or collective proceeding: the payment is held, the client is told, ops refunds it or allocates it with the judge's authorisation.
13. A bank return or chargeback undoes the allocation on the return date, cancels the receipt and informs the client.
14. Every payment linked to a file gets a French PDF receipt, versioned, with no remaining balance until M3 computes interest.
15. The payer's name is on the receipt only if the payer is a party to the file; otherwise « versement d'un tiers ».
16. No go-live with real payments before M3 computes interest; M3 re-runs earlier allocations by date.
17. The client is told of every payment we allocate (instalments of a known plan included) and of every payment held for its file.
18. Imports, assignments, refunds and reversals: admins and ops managers; collectors see the payments of their portfolios; clients see the payment feed, not bank lines.
19. Synthetic CAMT.053 and CSV files only, until 2–3 anonymised real exports from French banks arrive to calibrate matching.
20. Bank fees and other debits on the dedicated account are not booked in M2b (explained gaps); the bank is asked to charge fees to the operating account.

## Consequences
- Client money is closed in every entry, so Σ5120 + Σ4710 + Σ4670 = 0 at every date by construction (I8); the property machine checks it after every step against Postgres.
- Overpaid, held and unidentified money stays in pooled 4710, attributed by `payment_id`; M2c remits from 4670 only, so it can never be remitted by mistake.
- Every payment entry is posted through one set-based function: M2b-2 … M2b-6 write the input tables and never insert ledger rows themselves (static test on the worker unchanged).
- Per-entry checks at commit cost a few index lookups per payment entry; M2b-4 measures them on 50k allocations (design risk R1, fallback: statement-level checks).
- Confirmation now also issues references: the 50k confirmation job went from ~20 s to ~23–27 s locally (50k references ≈ 2.6 s, of which ≈ 1 s is the change log; the extended commit-time check adds ≈ 0.3 s for 50k openings). The migration's back-fill of 50k references takes ≈ 3 s.
- A correction that would take an allocated receivable below zero is refused (`LG007`): the correction must first reverse the allocation.
